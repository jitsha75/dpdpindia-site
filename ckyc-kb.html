CKYC 2.0 — The New Central KYC Registry
Complete Workflow & Regulatory Guide

From 1 August 2026, CERSAI's Central KYC Registry runs on a revamped architecture — real-time APIs, OTP-based per-download consent, AI facial de-duplication, and mobile-number search. This guide explains exactly how it works, how it differs from Aadhaar eKYC, and what it means for AUA/KUA and every Reporting Entity.

Published9 August 2026
LevelRegulatory / Operational
Read Time~20 min
CoverageWorkflow · AUA/KUA · CERSAI · UIDAI · Volumes
Back to Aadhaar & KYC Processes

CKYC and Aadhaar eKYC are frequently conflated but serve distinct purposes. eKYC (via UIDAI's AUA/KUA/Sub-KUA framework) verifies a customer's identity live, at the moment of first onboarding. CKYC (via CERSAI's registry) stores that completed record once and makes it reusable across every regulated financial institution the customer subsequently deals with. CKYC 2.0 does not replace eKYC — it changes how fast, how securely, and how consensually a KYC record is found, retrieved, and reused after it has first been created.

Section 01

What Changed on 1 August 2026

CKYC 2.0 is a joint initiative of CERSAI (registry operator) with RBI, SEBI and IRDAI as the regulators of the entities that feed and consume the registry. It upgrades an existing PMLA obligation (Rule 9(1A) of the PML Rules, 2005) — it is not new law.

DimensionCKYC 1.0 (pre-August 2026)CKYC 2.0 (from 1 August 2026)
Data transportBatch XML files over SFTPReal-time JSON REST APIs
Search methodsPAN or KIN onlyPAN, KIN, and registered mobile number
Consent for retrievalImplicit RE-level authorisationMandatory OTP-based, per-event consent to the customer's registered mobile before each download

Design Intent

Mobile-number search increases the odds a record is actually found; OTP-per-download gives the customer an auditable consent trail every time a new institution pulls their record; AI facial de-duplication targets synthetic and duplicate identities at the registry level — all broadly consistent with DPDP Act 2023 consent and purpose-limitation principles.

Section 02

Institutional Architecture — CERSAI, RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, UIDAI

CKYC sits inside a layered regulatory structure. It is useful to separate the registry operator, the sector regulators, and the identity-verification layer (UIDAI) — CKYC 2.0 touches all of them without merging them.

LayerBodyRole
Registry operatorCERSAI — owns CKYCR (ckycreg.in / CKYCRR 2.0), issues the 14-digit KIN, hosts the API layer, enforces de-duplication and consent-notification logic.
Banking regulatorRBI — mandates CKYC for banks, NBFCs, payment/small finance banks; first-wave CKYC 2.0 adopter alongside insurers.
Securities regulatorSEBI — extends CKYC to brokers, DPs, mutual funds/AMCs (cross-linked via the KRA system); phased onto CKYC 2.0 later in 2026.
Insurance regulatorIRDAI — mandates CKYC for life/non-life insurers; in the first adopter wave with banks.
Identity layerUIDAI — operates Aadhaar authentication/eKYC (CIDR) through AUA/KUA/Sub-KUA/ASA. Not the CKYC registry — an upstream source of verified identity that REs later upload to CKYCR.

The structural point: UIDAI/AUA/KUA and CERSAI/CKYCR are two systems a Reporting Entity uses in sequence, not two names for one thing. A bank may use a KUA to run Aadhaar eKYC (identity verification) — and separately, as a distinct PMLA-mandated step, upload the resulting KYC record to CERSAI's CKYCR (registry storage and future reuse).

Section 03

Role of AUA and KUA in the CKYC 2.0 Process

AUA/KUA do not connect to CERSAI directly — CKYCR interfaces with Reporting Entities, not UIDAI-empanelled intermediaries. Their role sits upstream (first-time record creation) and downstream (whenever a CKYC search comes back empty).

Where AUA/KUA are invoked
First-time KYC creation
No existing KIN found → RE (as AUA/KUA, or via a Sub-KUA arrangement) performs Aadhaar eKYC → RE creates a CKYC record from that output and uploads to CERSAI within 3 working days → CERSAI issues a new KIN.
Where AUA/KUA are bypassed
Record reuse via CKYC search
Existing KIN found via PAN/KIN/mobile search → OTP consent to the customer → RE downloads the CKYC record directly from CERSAI. No eKYC call, no AUA/KUA involvement in this path at all.

Net effect on the AUA/KUA function

  • AUA/KUA remains mandatory the first time a customer's KYC record is created via Aadhaar — CKYC 2.0 does not bypass UIDAI's authentication or consent requirements.
  • AUA/KUA becomes optional for every subsequent onboarding of the same customer elsewhere — mobile-number search is built to intercept a growing share of onboarding events before they reach an eKYC call.
  • Sub-KUA arrangements (fintechs, smaller NBFCs, insurance intermediaries) are directly exposed, since Sub-KUA revenue is metered on eKYC calls, not CKYC searches.
  • KUAs retain a role creating new records, and increasingly as vendors of "CKYC-search-first, eKYC-fallback" middleware — a shift from pure eKYC provisioning to KYC orchestration.
Section 04

Impact on Volumes & the AUA/KUA Business Model

CKYC 2.0's core objective — eliminate repeat KYC across institutions — is structurally a demand-reduction event for Aadhaar eKYC volume, offset by demand elsewhere.

Downward pressure

Every successful CKYC reuse is one eKYC transaction that doesn't happen. Mobile-number search raises the CKYC "hit rate" versus PAN/KIN-only search. Banks and insurers — the first-wave adopters — will feel this earliest; MF/broker-focused Sub-KUAs have a longer runway.

Offsetting demand

First-time KYC creation is unaffected — India's financial-inclusion pipeline keeps generating fresh eKYC demand. AI de-duplication raises the premium on high-assurance (biometric) verification at first creation. A new service line opens: CKYC search-and-orchestration middleware, KIN-linkage APIs, OTP-consent tooling.

Net read: Sub-KUAs metered purely on eKYC transactions to banking/insurance clients are the most exposed; KUAs that reposition as CKYC-plus-eKYC orchestration providers are best placed. Pricing models built solely on eKYC-per-transaction volume should be revisited toward platform/subscription structures.

Section 05

How an End User Initiates a CKYC-Based Onboarding

First-time customer (no existing KIN)

  1. Approaches a bank, NBFC, insurer, or intermediary for onboarding.
  2. Completes identity verification via Aadhaar eKYC (OTP/biometric), V-CIP, or physical documents.
  3. Gives explicit consent to (a) use Aadhaar data for KYC and (b) create/upload a CKYC record to CERSAI.
  4. Institution uploads the record within 3 working days; CERSAI issues a 14-digit KIN.
  5. Customer may request their KIN for faster onboarding elsewhere in future.

Existing customer (KIN already exists)

  1. Provides PAN, existing KIN, or simply their registered mobile number to the new institution.
  2. The institution's system searches CKYCR by PAN/KIN/mobile match.
  3. On a match, CERSAI sends an OTP to the customer's registered mobile, seeking consent for this specific institution to retrieve the record.
  4. Customer confirms the OTP; the institution downloads the record — typically without re-collecting Aadhaar, PAN copies, or address proof.
  5. If details have changed, a Re-KYC update should be completed before the new institution relies on the record.

Section 07

Comparative Roles — CERSAI, UIDAI, AUA, KUA

AttributeCERSAI (CKYCR)UIDAI (CIDR)
Legal mandatePMLA Rules 2005, Rule 9(1A)Aadhaar Act 2016 + Regulations
What it operatesCentral KYC Registry — stores completed KYC recordsCentral Identities Data Repository — biometric/demographic database
Direct relationship withAll PMLA Reporting EntitiesAUA, KUA, ASA (via signed agreements)
Data it returnsFull KYC record tied to a KINAuthentication result (AUA) or KYC fields — never the Aadhaar number itself (KUA)
Consent (2026)OTP-based, per-event consent before every retrievalRegulation 7 — explicit, informed, specific, voluntary consent per authentication
  • UIDAI verifies who a person is, at first contact, through AUA/KUA acting as its licensed gateways.
  • AUA/KUA are the technical/contractual bridge between a Reporting Entity and UIDAI's identity data.
  • CERSAI stores the outcome of that verification (or any other valid KYC method) once and makes it reusable — CERSAI never touches Aadhaar biometric data itself.
  • RBI, SEBI and IRDAI sit above all three, mandating their Reporting Entities use both systems correctly and on schedule.
Section 08

Compliance Checklist for Reporting Entities

  • Confirm CKYC 2.0 API integration is live against CERSAI's revised real-time JSON schema.
  • Run CKYCR search (PAN/KIN/mobile) before triggering fresh eKYC or physical KYC collection.
  • Implement OTP-consent handling in onboarding UI/UX; log the consent event for audit.
  • Re-validate Sub-KUA/KUA vendor contracts against likely eKYC volume reduction.
  • Enforce Aadhaar masking end-to-end wherever Aadhaar-linked data is displayed or logged.
  • Ensure Re-KYC updates push to CKYCR promptly — updates now auto-propagate to every linked RE.
  • Brief the Principal Officer on the phased timeline: banks/insurers live from 1 Aug 2026; MF/broker dates to be tracked as SEBI finalises capital-markets rollout.
Open Questions to Track

Exact SEBI go-live date for MFs/brokers is not yet uniformly notified. Interaction between CKYC 2.0's OTP-consent model and the DPDP Act's Consent Manager framework is not fully reconciled in public guidance. AI facial de-duplication may produce false-positive duplicate flags — REs should have an escalation path.
