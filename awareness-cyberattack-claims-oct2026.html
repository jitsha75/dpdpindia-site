Reported Cyber-Attack and Data-Theft Claims Involving Indian Public Bodies

What we could verify, what we could not, and what organisations should do.

Published1 October 2026
CategoryAwareness Note · Cyber Incidents
Read time3 min
DPDP Act 2023 CERT-In Directions Data Breach Unverified Claims

Why we are writing this

On the evening of 30 September 2026 we received an unsolicited email, sent from a personal email address to a large number of media and corporate inboxes. It alleges that a cybercriminal using the alias ByteToBreach has put data stolen from several Indian entities up for sale. We have not been able to establish who sent it. We are sharing what we could and could not verify purely for public awareness, and in the hope that any affected organisation will come forward early.

What the email claims

  • ▸Source code, databases and files taken from Indian networks and offered on underground forums, together with the deployment of a ransomware strain that is said to be still under analysis.
  • ▸Victims said to include West Bengal Electronics Industry Development Corporation (Webel), three state governments, a central-government-linked technology body, an internet service provider and an emergency-medical-services provider.
  • ▸Data said to include government records, subscriber records, and staff, patient and KYC information, along with leftover cloud, virtualisation and code-repository access that could enable a second attack.
Treat as claims only

All of the above is a claim. We have not opened the leak sites or links cited in the email, we have not downloaded any material, and we do not recommend that anyone does.

What we could verify

  • Press reports by The Statesman and UNI confirm a malware attack on the West Bengal State Data Centre, which operates under Webel, between 9 and 13 September 2026. It reportedly went unnoticed until 13 September and disrupted services. The reports differ on the outcome: one says a vast amount of data was stolen, the other says no stolen information has been recovered.
  • ByteToBreach is an alias that security researchers, including KELA, have profiled as an active seller of stolen government, banking and airline data across several countries.

That is the extent of what we could confirm. We found no independent confirmation of the claims about the other named entities, and none linking the alias to the Webel incident. Please treat those claims as unverified.

Why it matters under Indian law

  • ▸Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023: Section 8(5) requires reasonable security safeguards, and Section 8(6) requires intimation of a personal data breach to the Data Protection Board and each affected Data Principal. The Schedule provides for penalties of up to Rs 250 crore for failing to take reasonable safeguards and up to Rs 200 crore for failing to give breach notice.
  • ▸DPDP Rules, 2025 prescribe the manner of breach intimation. Provisions commence in phases, so organisations should confirm which are in force for them.
  • ▸CERT-In Directions of 28 April 2022 require specified cyber incidents to be reported within six hours of noticing them. This is already in force.

What organisations should do now

  • Have your security team assess whether your data, credentials, source code or vendor access could be affected. Do this through your own logs and monitoring, not by downloading leaked files.
  • Rotate credentials, API keys and access to VPN, vCenter, cloud and git environments, and review vendor and contractor accounts.
  • Preserve logs and report suspected incidents to CERT-In (incident@cert-in.org.in).
  • Prepare to notify affected individuals, and warn staff and customers about phishing and KYC-fraud attempts that often follow data sales.

A request to organisations

If your organisation, or a client, is affected, or you hold verified information about these claims, please come forward: report to CERT-In and the concerned authority, and consider sharing publicly whatever can safely be shared so that others can protect themselves. Silence helps only the attacker. We will update this note if credible facts emerge, and we welcome corrections from any organisation named or described here, through our Contact page.

Sources

UNI, 22 September 2026: Massive cyberattack disrupts Bengal govt services, vast amount of data stolen
